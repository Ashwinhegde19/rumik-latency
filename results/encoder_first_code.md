# Mimi encoder: is frame-0 code 1049 content-independent?

Script: `eval/encoder_first_code.py` (`modal run`, Tesla T4, transformers 4.57.6, fp32 MimiModel from the pinned
rumik-oss-1 revision, `MimiModel.encode(..., num_quantizers=8)`). Raw data: `results/encoder_first_code.json`.
29 clips (9 rumik TTS samples, 5 audit clips, 5 synthetic signals, 10 LibriSpeech dev-clean utterances from
`~/Projects/MendSpeech_All_Plans/data`), 252 variants. Remote time 26 s, estimated cost about $0.01.

## Verdict

**The encoder does NOT emit 1049 for any input.** Frame-0 codebook-0 is 1049 in 135/252 variants overall, but only
9/112 when the first 80 ms is loud (RMS > -30 dBFS) versus 126/140 when it is quiet. Loud onsets produce 30+ different
frame-0 codes (430, 1116, 2018, 108, 995, 1692, ...). The 1 kHz tone gives 2018, white/pink noise give 430, trimmed
and mid-word speech cuts give ordinary speech codes. The 9 loud exceptions are 7 click-train variants (an 8-sample
click in an otherwise empty frame), one white-noise variant, and one TTS mid-cut whose frame 0 is -19 dBFS. So the
"regardless of content" half of H1 is refuted: 1049 is content-dependent and means "(near-)silence".

**But 1049 is not the plain silence code either; it is the silence code *at the zero-padded start*.** Digital silence
encodes to codebook-0 sequence `1049, 127, 1880, 1031, 1031, 1031, 1492, 2029, 2029, 2029, 2029, 1316, 1316, ...`:
an 11-frame (0.9 s) start-up transient before the steady-state silence code 1316. The same silent 80 ms that gives
1049 at frame 0 gives 1833/1934/1316/255/... when 12 frames of real silence are prepended (`pad12f`, frame 12).
1049 never occurs at frames 1-15 of any variant (0/3500+ frames), exactly as it never occurs after frame 0 in the TTS
output. The encoder-side transient `1049 -> 127 -> 1880` is precisely what the TTS reproduces: in the 320 generated
sequences codebook 0 is 1049 at frame 0 in 320/320 (never elsewhere), 127 at frame 1 in 266/320 (never elsewhere; 958
is the runner-up, 34/320, also frame-1 only), and 1880 at frame 2 in 185/320.

So the two hypotheses are both half right, and the combined explanation is: the training clips began with
(near-)silence (otherwise the LM could not have learned 1049 with 100% consistency, since loud starts encode to other
codes), and the specific values 1049/127/1880 are the causal encoder's boundary signature for silence (frame 0 sees
1920 real samples plus 5240 samples = 218 ms of zero padding, i.e. only 27% real audio, and the encoder transformer has no
past frames). The LM learned that signature as a rule. Practical consequence: the first 1-2 generated frames are a
codec artefact of "silence at t=0", not a modelled pause, which is consistent with speech starting around frame 3.

Decoded loudness: a 1049 frame decoded on its own has median -63.7 dBFS (max -20.9 dBFS for the one loud TTS
exception, so the acoustic codebooks can still carry energy); non-1049 frames decode to median -22 dBFS. Decoding
frame 0 alone and taking frame 0 of the full decode give identical numbers (causal decoder).

Surprises: (1) frames 1-7 of digital silence are themselves a fixed, fully deterministic start-up transient across all
8 codebooks (one identical 8-tuple in 60/60 silent-start variants), and steady state takes 0.9 s; (2) rumik's own
frame-0 tuples vary in codebooks 1-7 (95 distinct over 320 sequences) while digital silence always gives one tuple,
which suggests the training clips started with low-level room noise rather than digital zeros; (3) the TTS's
as-is outputs (which are Mimi decodes) re-encode to 1049/127 at frames 0/1 too, so the round trip is stable.
